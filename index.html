System

From floorplan to buildable interior

The room has to make sense before it can look good. This stays high level on purpose, because the point is the workflow, not the internals.

Step 01
Layout

Generate a furnished room layout that makes sense for the actual apartment and intended use.

Step 02
Validity

Check that the room is usable, coherent, and not just visually plausible.

Step 03
Matching

Connect the concept to real furniture and materials while preserving style, dimensions, and budget logic.

Step 04
Rendering

Produce the final interior image only after the room concept is grounded in real-world constraints.

In practice

The point is not to make a prettier render. It is to make a room someone can actually act on.

Real items
The furniture and materials should correspond to things a person can actually source instead of objects that only work inside the image.
Real constraints
Dimensions, layout coherence, and budget logic need to survive the generation process rather than getting washed out by a pretty render.
Real workflows
The output should help with furnishing, sourcing, and renovation instead of ending its life as marketing-only inspiration.
Moat

The hard part is not image generation. The hard part is making generated interiors real.

A stylish room is easy to fake. The hard part is making the room correspond to reality: real dimensions, real products, real budgets, and real constraints.

What the moat is
  • layout logic grounded in how real rooms work
  • checks that keep the room usable
  • matching across style, products, and constraints
  • real products instead of fake furniture
  • generation that stays inside real-world constraints
What the moat is not
  • not one prompt
  • not one diffusion model
  • not one impressive render
  • not generic “AI interior design” positioning
  • the moat is the full system that connects a generated room to the real world
Wedge

Start where the pain is clearest: empty apartments

We start with empty apartments because that is where the gap between inspiration and execution is most obvious. Someone has to turn a blank space into a room that can be sold, rented, or lived in.

Why this starting point works

  • obvious before/after value
  • repeatable input format
  • direct link to furnishing and renovation decisions

Who feels the pain first

  • developers and furnishing operators
  • brokers and leasing teams
  • people trying to get a unit move-in ready

Where it can expand

  • furnishing packages
  • sourcing workflows
  • renovation planning
  • transaction layer around implementation
Opportunity

What starts as interior generation can become an execution layer

If this works, the company does not stop at rendering. It becomes part of the path from design intent to furnishing, sourcing, pricing, and transactions.

Now

Generate interiors that can actually be built

Make generated interiors useful when someone actually has to furnish the room.

Next

Connect design to furnishing and sourcing

Help people choose products, price rooms, and prepare the handoff into implementation.

Later

Become a transaction layer for interiors

Move from generation into the infrastructure that connects room design to execution and purchase.
