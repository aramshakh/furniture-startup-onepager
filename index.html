Workflow

Treat the product like a system, not like a render prompt.

From floorplan to buildable interior

The room has to make sense before it can look good. The workflow should stay tied to constraints from the start.

01
Layout

Generate a furnished room layout that makes sense for the apartment and intended use.

02
Validity

Check that the room is usable, coherent, and not just visually plausible.

03
Matching

Connect the concept to real furniture and materials while preserving style, dimensions, and budget logic.

04
Rendering

Produce the final room image only after the room concept is grounded in real-world constraints.

Proof

The room has to survive contact with reality.

The hard part is not image generation

A stylish room is easy to fake. The hard part is making the room correspond to reality.

Layout logic

Validity layer

Real products

Grounded output

Wedge

Start where the pain is obvious: a blank apartment that has to become real fast.

Start where the pain is clearest: empty apartments

We start with empty apartments because that is where the gap between inspiration and execution is most obvious. Someone has to turn a blank space into a room that can be sold, rented, or lived in.

Why this starting point works

Obvious before/after value, repeatable inputs, and a direct link into furnishing and renovation decisions.

Who feels the pain first

Developers, furnishing operators, brokers, leasing teams, and anyone trying to get a unit move-in ready.

Where it can expand

Furnishing packages, sourcing workflows, renovation planning, and eventually a transaction layer around execution.
