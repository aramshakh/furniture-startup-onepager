System

Keep this high level. The important thing is the workflow shape, not the internals.

From floorplan to buildable interior

The room has to make sense before it can look good. That means treating the workflow like a system, not a render prompt.

01
Layout
Generate a furnished room layout that makes sense for the actual apartment and intended use.
02
Validity
Check that the room is usable, coherent, and not just visually plausible.
03
Matching
Connect the concept to real furniture and materials while preserving style, dimensions, and budget logic.
04
Rendering
Produce the final interior image only after the room concept is grounded in real-world constraints.
In practice

The value is not a prettier output. The value is a room a person can actually act on.

The point is not to make a prettier render. It is to make a room someone can actually act on.

The room has to survive contact with reality.
  • Real items. The furniture and materials should correspond to things a person can actually source instead of objects that only work inside the image.
  • Real constraints. Dimensions, layout coherence, and budget logic need to survive the generation process rather than getting washed out by a pretty render.
  • Real workflows. The output should help with furnishing, sourcing, and renovation instead of ending its life as marketing-only inspiration.
Moat

A stylish room is easy to fake. The hard part is making the room correspond to reality.

The hard part is not image generation. The hard part is making generated interiors real.

Real dimensions, real products, real budgets, real constraints. That is where the system has to hold.

Layout logic grounded in how real rooms workThe room should make spatial sense before anyone cares whether it looks stylish.
Checks that keep the room usableThe system needs a validity layer, not just a taste layer.
Matching across products, style, and constraintsGeneration has to stay attached to real choices instead of drifting into decorative fiction.
Real products instead of fake furnitureThe point is to connect the image to what can actually be bought, priced, and implemented.
What the moat is not

Not one prompt. Not one diffusion model. Not one impressive render. The moat is the full system that connects a generated room to the real world.

Wedge

Start where the pain is obvious: a blank apartment that needs to become real fast.

Start where the pain is clearest: empty apartments

We start with empty apartments because that is where the gap between inspiration and execution is most obvious. Someone has to turn a blank space into a room that can be sold, rented, or lived in.

Why this starting point works

Obvious before/after value. Repeatable input format. Direct link to furnishing and renovation decisions.

Who feels the pain first

Developers and furnishing operators. Brokers and leasing teams. People trying to get a unit move-in ready.

Where it can expand

Furnishing packages. Sourcing workflows. Renovation planning. A transaction layer around implementation.
